## Boolean and derived dimensions (12)

These fields are true or false and do not carry a selected value.

| dimension | values | description |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| `organic` | — | The product is organic: it is sold as organic, or is grown or made from organically produced ingredients, and that is a reason to buy it. A certification mark is not required; an explicit organic claim on the product is enough. Answer true or false. Not applicable for: products described only as natural, herbal, chemical-free or pure without saying organic, or where 'organic' appears only in the brand name and not as a claim about the product. |
| `eco_friendly_packaging` | — | A deliberate shift from the shelf’s usual disposable plastic packaging to glass, metal, paper, card, cloth or certified compostable material, including durable returnable or refillable containers. | `d2c_brand` | — | A brand created primarily as an online or direct-to-consumer business that still sells mainly through its own digital channels or online marketplaces rather than conventional retail. This describes the brand’s route to market across food, care, household goods, apparel or electronics, without implying premium pricing. An established mass-retail brand, marketplace reseller or white-label storefront does not qualify merely because it also sells online. |

This can describe foods, baby and personal care, supplements, cosmetics and fragrance, decor and furnishings, kitchenware and cleaners, devices or pet products. A foreign brand’s locally manufactured or assembled variant is not an imported SKU. |

## Personalization

Every dimension above personalizes. None is product-side only \- if we know a key for a user, it changes their feed. What differs is WHAT KIND of thing the key is.

**Facts about the user.** Something true of the person or the household: a nut allergy, oily skin, a toddler, a cat, an induction cooktop. Learned once, changing rarely.

**Preferences of the user.** Something they have shown they like: organic, Korean food, cast iron, premium labels, trying whatever is new. Learned from repeats and held as a degree rather than a yes.

The user model is one flat `(dimension, value?) -> affinity` map. A preference can sit at either grain: `cuisine_style:korean` is a preference for a value, while `new_to_indian_retail` has no value and the dimension key itself is the preference \- for novelty, rather than for any particular novel thing. No separate store is needed; the grain is a property of the dimension. Some preferences transfer across their own values \- a user who bought aloe vera should be shown other natural ingredients \- while others may match only the exact value.

The mission axes personalize the same way; their kinds are in `docs/mission-axes-by-category.md`.
